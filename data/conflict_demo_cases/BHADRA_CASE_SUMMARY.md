# BenchSplit Legal Conflict Demonstration

## Legal issue
Whether participation in arbitration, consent recorded in procedural orders, or failure to object amounts to waiver under Section 12(5) of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996.

## Case 1: Delhi High Court
Bhadra International India Pvt. Ltd. v. Airports Authority of India
Citation: 2025 SCC OnLine Del 698
Decision date: 11 February 2025

The Delhi High Court upheld the arbitration award. It treated the parties' request for appointment, recorded consent, and participation without objection as relevant to the question of waiver.

## Case 2: Supreme Court
Bhadra International (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. Airports Authority of India
Citation: 2026 INSC 6
Decision date: 5 January 2026

Key paragraphs: 79, 81, 87, 123 to 125.

The Supreme Court held that waiver under Section 12(5) requires a clear and unequivocal express written agreement after disputes have arisen. Mere conduct or procedural participation cannot establish such waiver.

The Supreme Court set aside the Delhi High Court judgment and the arbitral awards.

## BenchSplit demonstration
1. Extract the legal issue from both judgments.
2. Compare their reasoning on express written waiver.
3. Identify the difference in their treatment of consent and participation.
4. Trace the Supreme Court decision as the subsequent controlling resolution.
5. Display the original High Court reasoning and the Supreme Court correction.

## Classification
This is a vertical precedent disagreement and Supreme Court resolution example. It is not a verified conflict between two High Courts.
